## 1. The objects

Doud and Moore (*J. Number Theory* 118 (2006) 62–70; arXiv math/0405534) proved that the minimal prime conductor of an even icosahedral Galois representation is 1951 and listed the six such representations of prime conductor below 10,000. Their objects are projective: continuous homomorphisms ρ̃: Gal(Q̄/Q) → PGL₂(C) with image A₅, each attached to a quintic field; a representation is *even* when, for a linear lift ρ, det ρ(τ) = 1 (τ complex conjugation) — equivalently the quintic field is totally real. By Tate's theorem every such ρ̃ lifts to a linear representation, and Doud–Moore note the lift "can be chosen to be ramified only at those primes at which ρ̃ is ramified." The two smallest conductors and their quintics, which are theirs:

  x⁵ − x⁴ − 780x³ − 1795x² + 3106x + 344  (conductor 1951),
  x⁵ − x⁴ − 856x³ + 4025x² + 28501x − 40877  (conductor 2141).

This deposit concerns, for each N ∈ {1951, 2141}, the linear lifts ρ: Gal(Q̄/Q) → GL₂(C) of Artin conductor exactly N. Their determinant is a Dirichlet character χ of order 5 modulo N (χ(3) = ζ₅ for N = 1951, 3 being a primitive root; χ(2) = ζ₅ for N = 2141), and their image is isomorphic to SL₂(F₅) × C₅, of order 600. There are exactly two lifts of conductor N, a representation and its complex conjugate, which differ by the twist by χ̄; since χ has odd order, χ̄(−1) = 1, so both take the same value at complex conjugation. That value, +I or −I, is therefore a datum of the conductor-N representation and not of the projective one (a twist by an odd character reverses it, at the cost of the conductor). *[This paragraph's argument was supplied during drafting on 2026-09-12; it is elementary and not in the earlier records.]* The records determine the value as **−I** at both conductors, in two ways: numerically, on a parity axis pre-registered before the run (AMENDMENT 1, 2026-07-27; selection 2026-08-03 for 1951, 2026-08-04 for 2141), and from the explicit construction of 2026-08-04, whose SL₂-valued lift has +I at complex conjugation and is carried to the selected lift by a character of order 10 whose parity is odd (its quadratic part is χ₋₁₉₅₁, resp. χ₋₂₄). Hence the archimedean factor is Γ_R(s+1)² and the conjectural Maass form is odd. Below, ρ denotes the conductor-N lift of determinant χ (the other is its conjugate), K̃ the field it cuts out (degree 600), and K̃₀ ⊂ K̃ the degree-120 field cut out by its determinant-one twist ρ ⊗ χ², whose image is SL₂(F₅); the projective ρ̃ is not used further.

By the Artin conjecture, L(s, ρ) is entire; by Booker's theorem (*Ann. of Math.* 158 (2003) 1089–1098: a two-dimensional irreducible Artin representation over Q that is not automorphic has infinitely many poles), entirety — indeed finiteness of the set of poles — is equivalent to the strong form: ρ corresponds to a Maass cusp newform of weight 0 on Γ₀(N) with nebentypus χ, Laplace eigenvalue exactly ¼, odd under x ↦ −x, whose Hecke eigenvalues are a_p = tr ρ(Frob_p). For two-dimensional representations over Q the conjecture is known in the dihedral case (Hecke, Maass), the tetrahedral and octahedral cases of either parity (Langlands 1980; Tunnell 1981), and for all odd representations (Khare–Wintenberger 2009, with Kisin); the even icosahedral case is the one that remains open. Booker, Lee and Strömbergsson (*J. London Math. Soc.* 102 (2020) 1067–1134; arXiv:1803.06016) list the even non-dihedral representations of conductor ≤ 2862 "assuming Artin's conjecture" (their Theorem 1.2) and remark, of their unconditional Theorem 1.1 on Selberg's eigenvalue conjecture, that "the first even icosahedral representation occurs at conductor N = 1951, so the bounds in Theorem 1.1 cannot be improved unconditionally beyond 1950." The conjecture is open for both representations treated here.

## 2. What is deposited

(i) **Hecke eigenvalue tables**: a_p = tr ρ(Frob_p) for every prime p < 10⁵ (9,591 unramified primes per conductor, plus the ramified value a_N with |a_N| = 1: a_N = ζ₁₀² for 1951, ζ₁₀⁸ for 2141), each entry exact in Z[ζ₅] (of the form ±ζ₁₀^k·{1/φ, 1, φ, 2} or 0, φ = (1+√5)/2), with the Frobenius class in the quintic's splitting field, χ(p), and a provenance tag beside it. The projective part of each a_p is fixed by the quintic (Frobenius class from the factorization of the polynomial modulo p). The remaining choice — which lift, i.e. the twist class, and with it the parity of §1 — was fixed in two ways that agree. One is a pre-registered, sealed numerical selection against the functional equations of L(s, ρ) and its twists (records of 2026-08-03 and 2026-08-04). The other is an explicit construction of the spin lift from the quintic by the trace-form method of Serre (*Comment. Math. Helv.* 59 (1984) 651–676) and Crespo (*J. Algebra* 127 (1989) 452–461), run openly against the sealed selection on 2026-08-04: it reproduced the selected values at 65 of 65 test primes at each conductor, exactly modulo one global quadratic character per field (the construction's pre-declared freedom). The construction record discloses that Crespo's published formula was not consulted in-session; the implemented element γ = det(I + U) was taken from the recalled structure of those papers and its correctness was established by internal tests (γ a non-square at all 221, resp. 231, involution-class primes tested, as the unique involution of SL₂(F₅) requires; the improper-orthogonal vanishing of det(I + U′) on odd orderings), not by citation. The full tables were generated from the resulting exact description on 2026-08-06 and their provenance column upgraded on 2026-08-09. Two kinds of entry are not produced by the construction and are tagged accordingly: the ramified value a_N at each conductor, selected among the tenth roots of unity by the twisted functional equations (`twist-determined`), and, at 2141 only, a₂, which the construction's route does not reach and which was fixed by the functional-equation instrument (`FE-resolved`); at 1951 every unramified entry, p = 2 included, is algebraic.

(ii) **Candidate Maass forms** built from the tables,

  F_N(z) = √y · Σ_{n≥1} c_n K₀(2πny) (e(nx) − e(−nx)),

with c_n multiplicative, c_p = a_p, c_{p^{k+1}} = a_p c_{p^k} − χ(p) c_{p^{k−1}} for p ≠ N, c_{N^k} = a_N^k; with the scripts that evaluate them and test their automorphy.

(iii) **The adjoint L-functions** L(s, Ad ρ), their values at s = 1, the scripts (Python coefficient generation; PARI/GP `lfun`), and the Heilbronn-type bound of §3(d) with its integer configurations, its certificate, and the replication's output.

(iv) **A SHA-256 manifest** of every file, and a pointer to the full working archive — Zenodo DOI 10.5281/zenodo.22727504 (all versions; the snapshot of 2026-09-12 is version 0.1.0, DOI 10.5281/zenodo.22727505; GitHub: wlenchus/TEP-archive) — which holds the pre-registrations, sealed commitments, verification passes, errata, and session records from which this statement is compressed.

## 3. What was computed and tested

The word *test* is used throughout for a finite-precision computation at finitely many points; nothing here is an interval-arithmetic proof, and the word *certificate* is reserved for exact arithmetic (§3(d)) and for file names that keep the records' usage.

(a) **Pointwise automorphy.** For γ = [[a, b], [c, d]] ∈ Γ₀(N) the candidate should satisfy F(γz) = χ(d) F(z). On 2026-08-09, for 18 generator–point pairs at N = 1951 (d ∈ {2, 3, 7}) the worst relative residual was 1.311×10⁻²⁴, and 1.120×10⁻²⁴ for 15 pairs at N = 2141 (3 of 18 dropped by the pre-registered tail rule, not stretched); two further pairs at 1951 with d ∈ {11, 23}, completing all four non-trivial values of χ(d), passed at 1.4×10⁻³² and 3.1×10⁻³³. Controls on that day: the alternative branch F(γz) = χ̄(d)F(z) fails at 1.90; a dihedral form of conductor 229, automorphic by Hecke–Maass, passes the same instrument at 1.05×10⁻²⁶, and the same form with one coefficient flipped fails at 1.99; flipping one committed bit of the icosahedral tables (b₁₀₇ at 1951, b₁₀₁ at 2141) moves the residual by 10–11 orders at the prescreen, and flipping a₁₁₃ at 1951 gives 0.341 in the 34-digit instrument. On 2026-09-04 the test was extended to complete generating sets produced by PARI/GP's `mspolygon`, reduced to distinct generators up to sign and inverse: for Γ₀(1951), 327 (the translation, 324 hyperbolic, 2 elliptic of order 3); for Γ₀(2141), 359 (the translation, 356 hyperbolic, 2 elliptic of order 2). The test points are the symmetric points maximizing min(Im z, Im γz) = 1/|c|, with the series truncated at 10⁵ terms and a rigorous tail bound computed per generator. At 1951, 305 of the 326 non-trivial generators have residual below 10⁻⁷ on the χ(d) branch and the largest residual is 9.0×10⁻⁴; for 257 generators the tail bound lies below the residual, so the residual is the measurement; for 69 the tail bound exceeds the residual (the pass is then "to the floor"), and for 9 of these — those with |c| ≥ 13N, the two elliptic generators included — the bound exceeds 1, so those passes are heuristic (small residuals, truncation not proven small). At 2141, 333 of 358 are below 10⁻⁷, the largest is 4.4×10⁻³, 284 are measurement-limited, 74 floor-limited, 10 of them (|c| ≥ 13N) with bound above 1; the two elliptic generators there (|c| = 5N) pass at 3×10⁻¹⁰ and 8×10⁻¹⁰ against floors of 6×10⁻⁷. The χ̄(d) branch fails at O(1) on every generator with χ(d) ≠ 1 and coincides with the χ(d) branch on exactly the generators whose d lies in ker χ (76 at 1951, 23.3%; 73 at 2141, 20.4%; an order-5 character predicts about one fifth). Phase-scrambled controls (a_p rotated by random phases, then extended multiplicatively) were run on the first 11 generators at each conductor and fail at O(1) on all of them (0.47–1.81; 1.01–1.96). A 34-digit pass with exact coefficients and a 60,000-term truncation, on a sample of 24 generators of Γ₀(1951) (16 with c = ±N, 4 with c = ±2N, 2 with c = ±3N, both elliptic), was 11 of 24 complete at drafting: residuals between 2.8×10⁻³¹ and 1.9×10⁻²⁹; the computed truncation tails lie between 4.6×10⁻³¹ and 8.2×10⁻³⁰, and seven of the eleven residuals exceed their tail by factors up to 17, which is consistent with rounding accumulated over the sum at 34 digits and means the residuals, not the tails, are the measurement. The file as completed at deposit (n of 24, stated in it) is in the kit. [The 2026-09-04 record's figures "300 below 10⁻⁷", "70 generators, 21.5%", "every generator tested" for the scrambled controls, and "each at or below its truncation floor" for the 34-digit pass are corrected here from the output files; erratum filed 2026-09-12.]

(b) **Fricke involution.** F(−1/(Nz)) is proportional to the form with complex-conjugate coefficients, with a constant of modulus 1, constant across test points to 1.3×10⁻²⁶ (1951) and 1.9×10⁻²⁶ (2141). On 2026-08-09 the constant was identified with the algebraic number τ(χ)/(√N · a_N), τ(χ) the Gauss sum (τ(χ)⁵ computed exactly in Z[ζ₅] from Jacobi sums): at 1951 a fresh 40-digit measurement agrees with it to 1.3×10⁻³⁹; at 2141 the same expression, with that field's χ and a_N and no other input, predicted a constant measured earlier to 5.7×10⁻¹², the two rival transfer rules failing at O(1). This is the Atkin–Lehner–Li shape of the Fricke pseudo-eigenvalue for a newform of prime level equal to the conductor of its nebentypus; it is identified numerically, not derived. In the convention of the records, F|W = −ε·G, the constant is minus the root number ε of Λ(s, ρ), and it supersedes the six-decimal value ε₁₉₅₁ ≈ 0.885096 − 0.465408i obtained in the selection of §2(i). Note that covariance of the candidate under z ↦ −1/(Nz) on the imaginary axis is equivalent, by Mellin inversion across the critical strip, to the entirety of Λ(s, ρ): a pole of Λ would contribute a residue term that the covariance excludes. Covariance at general points, which is what was tested, is the stronger statement. Either way the test is a finite-precision reading of the conjecture's content at the tested points, not a proof of it.

(c) **The adjoint L-function.** L(s, Ad ρ) was assembled from the tables (at unramified p the local factor is (1 − X)⁻¹(1 − t_pX + X²)⁻¹ with t_p = |a_p|² − 2; at p = N the inertia-invariant line gives (1 − X)⁻¹ and conductor exponent 2) with conductor N², archimedean factor Γ_R(s)³ (Ad ρ(τ) = I), and root number +1 — a theorem, since Ad ρ is orthogonal and the Artin root number of an orthogonal representation is +1 (Fröhlich–Queyrut, *Invent. Math.* 20 (1973) 125–138; Deligne, *Invent. Math.* 35 (1976) 299–316; the archive's citation card is the record of 2026-07-26, which read Deligne's Theorem 1.5 and Fröhlich–Queyrut's Theorem 3). PARI's `lfuncheckfeq`, which compares the two sides of the theta relation implied by the functional equation, returns a residual of 2⁻⁵³ at both conductors; the opposite sign returns 2¹. The check is evidence about the analytic shape of L(s, Ad ρ): a pole in the strip, or a wrong conductor, gamma factor or coefficient at a small prime, would break the theta relation at double precision. The values

  L(1, Ad ρ₁₉₅₁) = 0.75804821636215188895388752332023…
  L(1, Ad ρ₂₁₄₁) = 0.70613628733255158349514330347510…

exist and are nonzero unconditionally (Brauer; non-vanishing of Artin L-functions at s = 1), but their digits as printed are those of `lfun`, whose algorithm assumes the functional equation and entirety — what the check supports and what the Artin conjecture for ρ would imply (Gelbart–Jacquet). Unconditionally (Brauer meromorphy, Wiener–Ikehara), Σ_{n≤X} |c_n|² ~ c·X with c = L(1, Ad ρ)/ζ(2) · N/(N+1): predicted 0.460602 and 0.429079; measured at X = 10⁵, 0.46120 and 0.42897. This is a consistency check of the tables at the level of their second moment, not evidence for the conjecture: the same identities hold for any Galois-type coefficient stream, automorphic or not.

(d) **A Heilbronn-type bound.** For a finite Galois extension L/Q with group G and a point s₀ ≠ 1, write θ_{s₀} = Σ n_π π for the Heilbronn character, n_π = ord_{s₀} L(s, π) over the irreducible representations π of G, so that θ_{s₀}(1) = ord_{s₀} ζ_L; the Artin conjecture at s₀ is the statement that all n_π ≥ 0. For a subgroup H and an irreducible character τ of H whose Artin L-function over L^H is known to be entire, ⟨θ_{s₀}, Ind_H^G τ⟩ = ord_{s₀} L(s, τ, L/L^H) ≥ 0 (Foote–Murty, *Math. Proc. Cambridge Philos. Soc.* 105 (1989) 5–11). Take first G = SL₂(F₅), L = K̃₀, whose two-dimensional representation "2" is the determinant-one twist ρ ⊗ χ². Every proper subgroup of SL₂(F₅) is solvable and every irreducible character of every proper subgroup has a known-entire L-function — Hecke for the linear and monomial ones (Q₈, Dic₃, Dic₅ are M-groups), Langlands' tetrahedral theorem, over the quintic field, for the three faithful two-dimensional characters of SL₂(F₃) — so the inequalities available from subgroups are exactly 33 distinct rows (32 monomial, one tetrahedral) from the 76 subgroups in 12 conjugacy classes. Booker (*Experiment. Math.* 15 (2006) 385–407, §2) observed that the monomial rows do not force n₂ ≥ 0: "the representations of dimension 2 and one of dimension 4 can hide a pole at a zero of L(s, ρ)" — his ρ being the six-dimensional representation, the configurations −[2] + [6], −[2′] + [6] and −[4′] + [6] — so that his group-theoretic criterion "unfortunately does not apply to checking the icosahedral case"; these configurations survive the tetrahedral row as well. The record of 2026-09-04 sharpens this quantitatively: an exhaustive integer search and a linear program over the 33 rows give ord_{s₀} ζ_{K̃₀} ≥ 4m for a pole of L(s, 2) of order m; at m = 1 exactly three configurations attain 4, namely {2: −1, 6: +1}, {2: −1, 2′: +1, 4′: +1} and {2: −1, 2′: −1, 4′: −1, 6: +2}; without the tetrahedral row the minimum falls to 2, so Langlands' theorem over the quintic field is load-bearing; the unique configuration of total order 2 in the full cone is −[4′] + [6], a pole of the four-dimensional L-function, not of L(s, 2). The linear program was replicated by a separate implementation in the same model family (explicit matrices, Burnside–Dixon character tables, separate LP; minimum 4.000). The same record extends the computation to the full group G = SL₂(F₅) × C₅ and L = K̃ (228 rows, the Goursat diagonals added; exhaustive at total order ≤ 4), finding the same three configurations in the sector of ρ and, for real s₀, a doubling to ≥ 8; this extension and the configuration list are single-implementation computations.

*[Supplied during the adversarial read of 2026-09-12 (same model family) and hand-verified by the drafter; it makes the bounds theorems with a displayed proof that does not rest on the linear program.]* The bound has an exact certificate. With the rows Ind_{C₅}λ = 2 + 3′ + 4 + 4′ + 5 + 6, Ind_{C₁₀}1 = 1 + 3 + 3′ + 5, Ind_{C₁₀}ξ = 2 + 4′ + 6, Ind_{C₁₀}ξ′ = 3 + 4 + 5 and the tetrahedral row Ind_{SL₂(F₃)}τ = 2 + 2′ + 6 (ξ faithful of order 10, ξ′ of order 5; dimensions 24, 12, 12, 12, 10 as the indices require), one checks character by character that

  2·Ind_{C₅}λ + Ind_{C₁₀}1 + 2·Ind_{C₁₀}ξ + 2·Ind_{C₁₀}ξ′ + 2·Ind_{SL₂(F₃)}τ = reg_{SL₂(F₅)} + 4·[2].

Pairing with θ_{s₀} and using ⟨θ, reg⟩ = θ(1) gives θ_{s₀}(1) + 4n₂ ≥ 0, i.e. ord_{s₀} ζ_{K̃₀} ≥ 4m. Over the full group, each row tensored with a character ψʲ of the C₅ factor is again a known-entire induced row (monomial, or tetrahedral over the quintic field), so the identity holds sector by sector: the mass of the sector containing ρ is at least 4m; every sector's mass is non-negative, being ⟨θ, Ind_{1×C₅}(1 ⊠ ψʲ)⟩, the order of a Hecke L-function of K̃₀; hence ord_{s₀} ζ_{K̃} ≥ 4m. For real s₀ the conjugate representation ρ̄ = ρ ⊗ χ̄ has a pole of the same order and lies in a different sector (χ ≠ 1), so ord_{s₀} ζ_{K̃} ≥ 8m. Consequently ord_{s₀} ζ_{K̃} ≤ 3 at a point s₀ ≠ 1 (≤ 7 if s₀ is real) implies L(s, ρ) holomorphic there, for poles of any order; Stark's theorem (*Invent. Math.* 23 (1974) 135–152) gives this from ord ≤ 1 for any Galois extension.

What is known in the literature, and what was checked: Foote–Wales (*J. Algebra* 131 (1990) 226–257) prove the order-2 statement under a solvability hypothesis, confirmed at secondary grade (the hypothesis is restated as Lemma 5.1 of arXiv:2107.03269 and in Gun–Hazra–Sahu, *Monatsh. Math.* 206 (2025) 853–883, whose own results are for solvable groups), and so does not apply to SL₂(F₅); Foote, *Trans. Amer. Math. Soc.* 321 (1990) 261–272, could not be read in-session and is unchecked — if it contained a general order-2 theorem it would remove only the configuration −[4′] + [6], not the bound 4m. The classification of SL₂(F₅) as not weak-almost-monomial (Cimpoeaș, arXiv:2409.05629; A₅ is) names the same obstruction abstractly. The literature on *Heilbronn characters* as objects of finite group theory — virtual characters whose restrictions to proper subgroups are genuine, and the minimal ones among them — studies exactly this object for general groups (Ginsberg, *J. Algebra* 331 (2011) 466–481; *Proc. Edinb. Math. Soc.* 56 (2013) 57–69; the survey Foote–Ginsberg–Murty, "Heilbronn characters", *Bull. Amer. Math. Soc.* 52 (2015) 465–496), and only its titles and abstracts could be read in-session; it is the likeliest place for the SL₂(F₅) configurations to appear, and the claim of no located precedent for this item is correspondingly weaker than for §3(a)–(c).

## 4. What is claimed, in three grades, and what is not

**Unconditional.** The tables of §2(i) as exact objects in Z[ζ₅] produced by the stated construction, with the two tagged exceptions disclosed there; their Frobenius classes and χ(p). The statement that the two conductor-N lifts share their value at complex conjugation (§1). The conductor N², gamma factor Γ_R(s)³ and root number +1 of L(s, Ad ρ). The existence and non-vanishing of L(1, Ad ρ) and the Rankin–Selberg asymptotic of §3(c). The bounds ord_{s₀} ζ_{K̃} ≥ 4m (≥ 8m for real s₀) of §3(d), proved by the displayed certificate over the theorems of Artin–Brauer–Hecke and Langlands, and the three configurations at m = 1 over SL₂(F₅), an exact finite enumeration replicated by a separate implementation. The identification of the tables with tr ρ(Frob_p) for the conductor-N lift of determinant χ rests on the agreement of the two methods of §2(i), one exact but verified internally rather than against the published formula, one numerical; the records grade it as established by that agreement (a wrong lift fits no single quadratic character on 65 primes), not as a proof in the literature's sense, and it remains open to the check of re-deriving the construction from Crespo's paper.

**Conditional on the Artin conjecture for ρ.** F_N is the Maass newform attached to ρ (unique with these Hecke eigenvalues, by strong multiplicity one); the Fricke identity of §3(b) with its algebraic constant holds exactly; the printed digits of L(1, Ad ρ) are exact digits.

**Numerical evidence, at finite precision and finitely many points.** Automorphy of F_N under complete generating sets of Γ₀(N) at the residuals and floors of §3(a), with the stated controls; the Fricke constant at 10⁻³⁹ and 10⁻¹² (§3(b)); the functional-equation residual 2⁻⁵³ for L(s, Ad ρ) (§3(c)). Each of these is consistent with the conjecture and inconsistent, at the stated precision, with the named alternatives (wrong branch, corrupted coefficient, scrambled phases, wrong root number).

**Precedent.** Searches on 2026-07-27 (arXiv, author pages, LMFDB; disclosed gaps: LMFDB's filtered query blocked, paywalled finals read as preprints, theses and slides invisible), 2026-08-29, 2026-09-04 and 2026-09-12 (the Booker papers and the Heilbronn literature, read against the arXiv preprints and publishers' abstracts; the citation cards are filed in the record of 2026-09-12) found no prior numerical treatment of even icosahedral Maass forms or of these L-functions, and no database entry for them: LMFDB's Maass forms (Seymour-Howell, *Res. Number Theory* 8 (2022), art. 83) cover trivial character only, with completeness verified for prime levels 2 ≤ N ≤ 67 and composite squarefree levels 6 ≤ N ≤ 105, and mention neither eigenvalue ¼ nor Artin representations; the published computations nearest in kind (Booker 2006; Booker–Strömbergsson, *J. reine angew. Math.* 607 (2007); Booker–Lee–Strömbergsson 2020) stop below conductor 1951 or treat other types; the odd-side computations of exotic weight-one forms (Child, arXiv:2201.08873 (2022), level ≤ 10⁴) do not reach the even case; Booker's theoretical note on Maass forms of icosahedral type (*Math. Z.* 292 (2019)) has no numerics; and the classification of even solvable eigenvalue-¼ forms (Uttenthal, arXiv:2512.20413 (2025)) excludes A₅ explicitly. Booker's 2006 paper states the reason in his setting: totally real A₅ fields "are very rare; the smallest known discriminant is far too large to test with current computers." The tests deposited here are of a different kind — automorphy of a candidate built from the Galois side, rather than spectral detection or verification through the L-functions of the splitting field — which is why conductor 1951 is within reach of a laptop; no comparison of the two approaches' standing is intended. The items of §3(a)–(c) are described as having no located precedent, subject to the searches' gaps; for §3(d) the qualification at the end of that section applies. This statement will be corrected on notice of a precedent.

**Not claimed.** The Artin conjecture for either representation; entirety of L(s, ρ) or of L(s, Ad ρ); the automorphy of F_N, which is tested, not proved; any strengthening of §3(d) beyond what subgroup-induced constraints give; priority for any method; and no claim is made with the words "first" or "unprecedented." By Booker's theorem L(s, ρ) has either no poles or infinitely many; everything above is consistent with the former alternative and decides nothing. No result here has been re-run by anyone outside the collaboration that produced it; "replication" in the records means a separate implementation within the same model family, never an independent one.

## 6. How to re-run

Python 3 with numpy, scipy and mpmath; PARI/GP 2.15. Every script takes its inputs by path relative to its own location, so the kit runs from any directory. The tables are `data/*.csv`. Generator sets: `generators/mspolygon_N.gp` (PARI, writes the raw side pairings, deposited as `generators/side_pairings_raw_N.txt`) and `scripts/dedup_generators.py N` (reduces them to distinct generators up to sign and inverse, 652 → 327 at 1951 and 716 → 359 at 2141; the reduced lists are deposited as `generators/generators_unique_N.csv` and `.npy`). The complete-generator tests run in about half a minute per conductor in double precision (`scripts/complete_generator_certificate.py`, `…_2141.py` — the file names keep the records' word; outputs `results/certificate_results*.tsv`) and about twenty to thirty minutes per generator at 34 digits (`scripts/certificate_mpmath_pass.py`; the rows completed at deposit are in `results/certificate_mpmath_results_partial.tsv`). The adjoint scripts regenerate the coefficients, write a PARI file into `results/`, and run `lfun` (`scripts/adjoint_L_and_RS_constant.py`, `…_2141.py`; outputs `results/adjoint_L_gp_output*.txt`). For the Heilbronn bound, `scripts/heilbronn_coin.py` builds SL₂(F₅) from the 120 unit icosians, finds all subgroups by closure, computes the induced rows and enumerates configurations of total order ≤ 4 exhaustively (about ten minutes; no linear program); `scripts/heilbronn_lp_replication.py` is the separate implementation (explicit matrices, Burnside–Dixon tables, LP and MILP); the certificate is `notes/HEILBRONN_CERTIFICATE.md`, checked by hand from the rows in `results/heilbronn_coin_output.txt`. On 2026-09-13 every script except the 34-digit pass was re-run from this layout and reproduced the deposited outputs exactly. `MANIFEST_SHA256.txt` lists every file.
